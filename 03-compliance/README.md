## What are regulatory requirements?

Regulatory requirements are laws or legally binding standards issued by governments. They define concrete obligations for handling data. Many focus on personal data, but regulations often extend to financial records, operational resilience and data sovereignty (where data is stored).

| Regulation | Jurisdiction | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GDPR | European Union / EEA | Personal data of individuals in the EU: lawful basis, data subject rights, breach notification, cross-border transfers |
| CCPA / CPRA | California, USA | Personal information of California residents: right to know, delete, opt out of sale or sharing |
| HIPAA | USA | Protected health information (PHI) held by covered entities and their business associates |
| PCI DSS | Global (industry standard, enforced contractually by card brands) | Cardholder data: storage, encryption, access control, network security |
| SOX | USA | Integrity of financial reporting and internal controls at public companies |
| Data residency / sovereignty laws | Country-specific | Where certain data must be stored and processed |

PCI DSS is an industry standard rather than a government law, but it is binding on organizations that process card payments.

## Regulatory compliance: the final audit

Compliance is the state in which the implementation of data security and privacy meets specific regulatory requirements. It is the "passed exam" of the data world: the process of proving to an external authority (a regulator, a partner or a customer) that your data protection practices actually work and align with the law.

## Note on data protection

Data protection is not a separate pillar. It is the operational outcome of applying [data security](../01-data-security/README.md) controls to enforce [data privacy](../02-data-privacy/README.md) requirements and to comply with the relevant data protection regulations and laws.
